1. Project Overview
This project asks you to connect the evolutionary computation techniques studied in this course (e.g., Genetic
Algorithms, Particle Swarm Optimization, Differential Evolution, and Multi-objective Evolutionary Optimization)
with emerging directions in artificial intelligence. You should select a frontier topic, understand the state of the
art through a focused literature review, identify a meaningful research opportunity, and develop and
experimentally evaluate an innovative solution. The interaction may go in either direction: evolutionary
computation may be used to enhance emerging AI technologies, or modern AI techniques may be used to improve
evolutionary algorithms. Hybrid approaches are also welcome.

2. Learning and Research Objectives
The project is intended to move beyond implementing a standard evolutionary algorithm on a conventional benchmark.
By completing the project, you should demonstrate the ability to:
•   understand and critically analyze recent research at the intersection of evolutionary computation and emerging AI;
•   formulate a clear and meaningful research problem;
•   design or adapt computational intelligence techniques to address that problem;
•   introduce a reasonable degree of innovation based on existing work; and
•   conduct rigorous comparative experiments and draw evidence-based conclusions.

3. Suggested Research Directions
The following topics are examples for inspiration rather than an exhaustive list. You are encouraged to explore other
frontier directions when they have a clear connection to evolutionary computation.
•   Evolutionary LLM architecture search
•   Evolutionary prompt engineering
•   Automatic algorithm design, discovery, or configuration
•   LLM-assisted evolutionary computation
•   Transformer-based or learned optimizers
•   Evolutionary multi-agent systems
•   Evolutionary fine-tuning and model adaptation & merging
•   Self-evolving AI systems
•   Multi-objective optimization for modern AI systems
•   Evolutionary optimization of agents or world models

4. Expected Research Depth
This is an open-ended research project. There is no single correct solution. A strong project should first establish what
has already been achieved in the selected area and then build on that understanding to make a meaningful technical
contribution.
Simply applying an off-the-shelf GA/PSO/DE implementation to a new dataset or task is generally insufficient for a
high mark. Innovation may arise from, for example, problem formulation, representation, fitness/objective design,

5. Literature Review Requirements
You must conduct a focused review of recent literature to establish the state of the art, identify limitations or gaps, and
motivate your proposed work.
•      Review at least 20 relevant papers published within the most recent three years (2024–2026).
•      The review should synthesize the literature by themes, methods, trends, strengths, limitations, or research gaps. It
       should not be a collection of paper-by-paper summaries.
•      The literature review should directly support the motivation and design of your proposed method.

6. Experimental Requirements
Experimental comparison is mandatory. Your experiments should provide convincing evidence about whether and
why the proposed approach works.
•      Include appropriate baselines, including relevant existing methods and/or conventional evolutionary approaches
       where applicable.
•      Use meaningful quantitative evaluation metrics that match the research problem.
•      For stochastic algorithms, conduct multiple independent runs where computationally feasible and report
       appropriate statistics (e.g., mean and standard deviation).
•      Experimental settings, datasets/tasks, model configurations, hyperparameters, and evaluation procedures should
       be described clearly enough to support reproducibility.

7. Required Deliverables
Submit all of the following by the deadline 15 November 2026:
1. Project Report (PDF): A self-contained research report covering the problem and motivation, literature review,
   methodology, experimental setup, results and analysis, discussion, conclusion, and references.
2. Source Code: Complete implementation used in the project, together with a README explaining environment
   setup, dependencies, data preparation, execution, and evaluation.
3. Experimental Data and Results: Raw or processed results sufficient to support the findings reported in the
   project, including relevant logs, result files, or evaluation outputs.
4. Supplementary Materials (if applicable): Additional configurations, prompts, visualizations, videos, model
   outputs, or other materials that help demonstrate or reproduce the work.
